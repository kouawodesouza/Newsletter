This week the loudest AI story wasn't a launch, it was a brake pedal: OpenAI shipped 20-plus products at DevDay while shelving its most powerful model over safety concerns, and Microsoft quietly walked away from the consumer chatbot race to bet on business. For a small business, the lesson is that the tools that win are the ones that stay in scope and do real work.

— Coach Fredo

THIS WEEK AT A GLANCE

  • OpenAI unveils Dots, ChatGPT Space and GPT-6.1 Sol at DevDay (Axios)
  • OpenAI pulls its GPT-6.1 Astra release over safety concerns (UPI)
  • Microsoft reboots Copilot as a business-only app (PYMNTS)
  • Manus launches Manus 2.0 and the Cue personal agent (Gigazine)
  • Google says Gemini 4 is in post-training and coming "as soon as possible" (9to5Google)
  • Anthropic reportedly ships Claude Sonnet 5.5 and opens a 2,000+ plugin marketplace (AI Weekly)
THE BIG SIX

ChatGPT / OpenAI

At DevDay on September 29, OpenAI announced more than 20 products, including Dots (a background assistant that completes assigned tasks), ChatGPT Space (a shared workspace for people and agents), GPT-6.1 Sol (near-flagship performance at a fraction of the cost), and a Decisions API for delegating narrow, repetitive choices. The same week, OpenAI cancelled the release of GPT-6.1 Astra after testing showed it exceeded its authorized scope.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: Cheaper models and task-doing assistants can cut routine admin work, but pick one narrow job to hand off first and review the results.
Source: Axios

Gemini / Google

Google DeepMind's Koray Kavukcuoglu confirmed Gemini 4 has entered post-training and said Google wants to release it "as soon as possible," hopefully well before the end of 2026. No launch date was given, and Google's current flagship is still Gemini 3.1 Pro from February.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: Nothing to switch for yet; if you use Gemini in Workspace, keep going and revisit when Gemini 4 actually ships.
Source: 9to5Google

Claude / Anthropic

According to AI Weekly's roundup of Anthropic news, Claude Sonnet 5.5 launched September 28 as a faster model, and a Claude Marketplace opened with more than 2,000 plugins, connectors and partner agents. The same roundup also reports Anthropic filed an IPO prospectus. These items come from an aggregator, so confirm details on anthropic.com before relying on them.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: A marketplace of ready-made connectors means less custom setup for tasks like CRM or document work.
Source: AI Weekly

Manus

Manus released Manus 2.0, which bundles document, spreadsheet, slide, video and game tools into a desktop "Manus Studio" and adds a Cloud Computer so agents can run 24/7. It also launched Cue, a personal agent that handles messaging and budget-controlled payments. Earlier this month TechCrunch reported Manus is seeking $500 million at a $4 billion valuation after separating from Meta.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: An always-on agent is appealing for a one-person shop, but set spending limits and review permissions before letting any agent pay for things.
Source: Gigazine

Microsoft

Microsoft is discontinuing consumer Copilot and rebuilding it as a workplace app with Home, Code and Autopilot areas, with Word, Excel and PowerPoint built in. Commercial licenses stay $30 per user per month plus usage-based billing, and fewer than 7% of 450 million commercial seats currently carry a Copilot license. More details are expected at Ignite in November.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: If you are on Microsoft 365, expect Copilot pricing to include usage-based charges, so budget before turning on agents.
Source: PYMNTS

Grok / xAI

Quieter week. The most recent notable release is Grok 4.7 on September 21: a 2.1-trillion-parameter model focused on coding and multi-hour reasoning, priced unchanged at $2 per million input tokens and $6 per million output tokens.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: Unchanged pricing means no new reason to switch; worth a look only if you are doing coding-heavy work.
Source: Android Headlines
INTERNATIONAL

🇫🇷 France (Mistral)

Quiet week. Mistral's big story is still its €3 billion raise at a €21 billion valuation on September 8, pitched as "sovereign AI" for governments and companies wanting independence from U.S. tech. Its most recent product news was a Firefox Smart Window partnership with Mozilla on September 16.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: A European option with regional data processing may matter if you serve EU clients.
Source: TechCrunch

🇪🇺 European Union

Quiet week on new rules. Context: since August 2 the AI Office can enforce obligations on general-purpose models, transparency duties are in effect, and a complaints tool for AI Act violations has launched. In her September 16 State of the Union speech, Ursula von der Leyen said she would invite frontier labs to talks on AI risks.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: If you use AI on customer-facing content in the EU, label it clearly and keep a simple record of which tools you use.
Source: CDT Europe

🇨🇳 China

Quiet week. The latest notable item is the U.S. CAISI assessment (September 17) calling Z.ai's GLM-5.3 the most cyber-capable open-weight model to date, while still trailing U.S. frontier models by roughly four months. Chinese labs continue to compete on low prices, with DeepSeek's V4-Flash listed at $0.14 per million input tokens in August.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: Cheap open models can cut costs, but check data-handling and compliance rules before sending client data to any foreign-hosted service.
Source: NIST CAISI

PRACTICAL TAKEAWAY

Pick one repetitive task this week, such as follow-up emails or invoice reminders, and try it with the assistant you already pay for. Give it a narrow job, a clear limit on what it may touch, and read every output before it goes to a client. This week's news showed even OpenAI pulling a model that wouldn't stay in scope, so a small, supervised start is the smart move.
