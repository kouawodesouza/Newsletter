This week the AI race quietly split into two lanes: price and trust. Anthropic shipped Claude Opus 5 at a lower cost than its predecessor while China's Moonshot AI rattled markets with a rival model at a fraction of US pricing — even as Google delayed its own flagship again and the EU switched on real enforcement teeth. For small businesses, the tools are getting cheaper and better, but "which one can I trust with client data" matters more than ever.

— Coach Fredo

THIS WEEK AT A GLANCE

  • Anthropic released Claude Opus 5, roughly doubling prior performance at the same price (Anthropic)
  • OpenAI rolled ChatGPT Health out to all US adults; health queries hit 300M/week (TechCrunch)
  • Google's Gemini 3.5 Pro still has no release date after missing three targets over reliability issues (Bloomberg)
  • Microsoft launched its first in-house cybersecurity AI model and a new "Perception" agent platform (TechCrunch)
  • The EU AI Act's enforcement powers kicked in, with fines up to €15M starting August 2 (The Next Web)
  • China's Moonshot AI rattled markets with Kimi K3, rivaling top US models at a fraction of the cost (Fortune)
THE BIG SIX

OpenAI / ChatGPT

OpenAI expanded ChatGPT Health to all US-based adults across every plan on July 23, letting users pull in data from Apple Health, Function, and MyFitnessPal for personalized health answers. Health-related queries on ChatGPT have jumped from 230 million to 300 million per week. The rollout came alongside a more natural GPT-Live-1 voice mode and tighter teen safety controls, even as the company weathered its fourth service outage in four days.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: If you're in wellness, fitness, or health-adjacent services, expect clients to arrive pre-informed (and sometimes misinformed) by ChatGPT — plan intake conversations accordingly.
Source: TechCrunch

Google / Gemini

Google's flagship Gemini 3.5 Pro has now missed three announced launch windows — June, then July 17 — and as of July 25 still has no public release date. Bloomberg reports DeepMind scrapped and rebuilt the base model after it fell short on hallucination rates and real-world reliability. The lighter Gemini 3.5 Flash, live since May, continues powering Search, Android, and Workspace for over 550 million monthly users.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: The Gemini tool you already use daily (Flash, in Search and Workspace) is stable and improving — no need to wait on the delayed Pro model to get value today.
Source: Tech Times / Bloomberg

Anthropic / Claude

Anthropic released Claude Opus 5 on July 24, holding pricing steady at $5/$25 per million tokens while roughly doubling Opus 4.8's performance on frontier benchmarks and landing within 0.5% of Claude Fable 5 at half the cost. New features in beta include mid-conversation tool switching and automatic safety fallbacks to alternate models.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: A cheaper near-frontier model means better AI-assisted drafting, analysis, and coding help without a price hike — worth re-testing if you shelved Claude over cost.
Source: Anthropic Newsroom

Manus AI

Tencent is in talks to become Manus' largest shareholder, leading a roughly $2 billion buyback after Chinese regulators forced Meta to unwind its planned acquisition of the agentic-AI startup. Manus, which moved its operations to Singapore, says it expects to remain operationally independent even under new Chinese ownership.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: If you've piloted Manus for autonomous task-running, the ownership uncertainty is worth watching — it can affect pricing, data policies, and long-term support.
Source: Bloomberg

Microsoft / Copilot & Azure AI

On July 27, Microsoft launched its first in-house cybersecurity model, MAI-Cyber-1-Flash, alongside a new agentic platform called Perception designed to find and remediate software vulnerabilities automatically. The launch follows April's restructured, non-exclusive OpenAI partnership and signals Microsoft building more of its own AI stack in-house.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: Cheaper, automated vulnerability scanning is trickling down from enterprise security budgets — ask your IT provider if Microsoft's new tools lower your security costs.
Source: TechCrunch

xAI / Grok

Elon Musk said on July 24 that Grok 4.6 will arrive in about two weeks, with Grok 4.7 following roughly two weeks after, alongside a new Grok add-in for Microsoft Outlook that drafts replies and summarizes email threads. Separately, xAI is facing a federal lawsuit from an Arkansas family alleging Grok generated abusive content involving their minor daughter — a reminder that safety guardrails still lag feature releases industry-wide.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: Before adopting any AI tool for client-facing work, check its safety and moderation track record, not just its feature list.
Source: Basenor / KARK News
INTERNATIONAL

🇫🇷 France

Microsoft and Mistral AI signed a "multibillion-dollar" deal on July 21 giving Azure customers in Europe access to Mistral's French data centers, with Mistral's Medium 3.5 and OCR 4 models now in Microsoft's Azure Foundry catalog. The deal comes as Mistral, backed by a €4B data center buildout, reportedly draws new investment interest from Samsung.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: European clients increasingly ask about data sovereignty — Mistral-on-Azure-in-France is a concrete answer if you serve EU customers who want data to stay on the continent.
Source: France 24

🇪🇺 European Union

The European Commission's AI Office gained sweeping new enforcement powers on July 28 to police how frontier AI labs manage systemic risk, with Article 50 transparency rules going live across all 27 member states and fines of up to €15 million or 3% of global turnover starting August 2. A German company was already fined €45 million for AI training-data violations.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: If you use AI tools with EU customers or data, August 2 is a real deadline — confirm your chatbots and AI features disclose they're AI, or you could be on the hook even as a small operation.
Source: The Next Web

🇨🇳 China

Beijing-based Moonshot AI released Kimi K3, a 2.8-trillion-parameter open-weight model that performed competitively with Claude Fable 5 and outperformed Opus 4.8 and GPT-5.6 at a fraction of the cost, rattling markets and sending rival Chinese AI stocks down sharply. It's the latest sign Chinese labs are closing the gap with US frontier models faster than expected.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: Cheap, capable open-weight models mean more affordable self-hosted AI options are coming — useful if data privacy or per-seat licensing costs have kept you on the sidelines.
Source: Fortune

PRACTICAL TAKEAWAY

This week's signal: prices are dropping and safety scrutiny is rising, often for the same tools. Before you roll out any new AI feature to clients this month, do two things — check if a cheaper model (Opus 5, Kimi K3) already covers your use case, and if you serve EU customers, confirm your AI disclosures are ready for August 2 enforcement. Speed matters less than picking tools you can defend if a client asks "is this safe and compliant?"
