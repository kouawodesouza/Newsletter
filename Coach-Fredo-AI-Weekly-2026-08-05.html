Meanwhile the Big Six shipped the unglamorous stuff that actually matters for small business: cheaper models, easier sign-ins, and usable voice AI.

THE BIG SIX

ChatGPT (OpenAI)

OpenAI is rolling out "Sign in with ChatGPT" in beta, letting users create or link accounts on partner apps — starting with Airtable, GitLab, HubSpot, Notion, Supabase, and Vercel — using their ChatGPT credentials instead of a separate signup. It's part of a broader push to make ChatGPT the front door to a growing ecosystem of connected business tools, alongside smaller changes like auto-converting large pastes into attachments to protect context windows.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: If you already use ChatGPT daily, this could mean one less password to manage when you connect it to apps like Notion or HubSpot — worth checking as these integrations roll out.
Source: OpenAI Help Center

Gemini (Google)

Google pushed Gemini 3.6 Flash and Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite to general availability, with 3.6 Flash improving token efficiency and agentic planning at a lower price than its predecessor, and Flash-Lite built for cheap, high-volume automation tasks.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: These are the models built for the boring, repetitive stuff — email triage, data entry, simple chatbots — at a price point that makes automating small tasks actually pencil out.
Source: Google AI for Developers

Claude (Anthropic)

Anthropic named Mariano-Florentino "Tino" Cuéllar — a former California Supreme Court justice and past president of the Carnegie Endowment for International Peace — as its first Chief Global Affairs Officer on August 4, reporting to president Daniela Amodei. The hire lands as Anthropic contests a Pentagon blacklisting in court and navigates a temporary export ban on its Mythos 5 and Fable 5 models, while separately deepening its enterprise partnership with Cognizant to embed Claude across client platforms.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: This is a policy and government-relations hire, not a product change — but it signals Anthropic expects AI regulation fights to intensify, which could shape what capabilities are available to you and when.
Source: Anthropic Newsroom

Manus

It's another quiet week for Manus — last week's reported Tencent-led buyback talks (after China blocked Meta's acquisition) remain unresolved, with no new product or ownership news since.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: No action needed — the product is unaffected for now, but keep an eye on the ownership situation before betting a workflow on it long-term.
Source: AInvest

Microsoft

Microsoft Research open-sourced Orchard, a framework that separates AI agent training from deployment, letting developers train agents in realistic sandboxed environments — coding, web browsing, tool use — before putting them into production tools, at a fraction of the cost of managed alternatives.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: This is a developer-facing tool, not something you'll touch directly — but it's a sign that building custom AI agents is about to get cheaper for the software vendors you already rely on.
Source: Microsoft Research

Grok (xAI)

xAI launched Grok Voice Think Fast 2.0, a new speech-to-speech voice model priced at $0.08 per minute of audio and now the default behind grok-voice-latest, alongside continued rollout of Grok across web, iOS, Android, X, and Google Workspace with new TinyFish and Exa plugins for live web search and retrieval.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: Cheap, fast voice AI is edging toward viable for things like appointment reminders or simple phone support — worth watching before you invest in a pricier voice vendor.
Source: Releasebot
INTERNATIONAL

🇫🇷 France

A Fortune feature published this week argues Mistral's push for "sovereign" European AI looks more appealing than ever, especially after reported U.S. restrictions on access to a top American model — even though Mistral still leans on American chips and cloud infrastructure to compete.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: If you're in Europe or serve European clients, data-sovereignty pressure could nudge more vendors toward EU-hosted AI options over the next year — worth asking your software providers where their models actually run.
Source: Fortune

🇪🇺 European Union

With the AI Act's enforcement powers now formally active, France's data protection authority (CNIL) moved fast — on August 4 it issued formal information requests to 14 financial institutions using AI-based credit scoring, demanding the Article 11 technical documentation required for high-risk systems. It's the first concrete national enforcement action since the EU AI Office's powers activated August 2.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: If you use any AI-based scoring, screening, or decision tool — even a third-party one — start documenting how it works now, before a regulator asks for it.
Source: Help Net Security

🇨🇳 China

Alibaba released Qwen3.8-Max this week, a 2.4-trillion-parameter model that reportedly matches or beats Anthropic's flagship on several benchmarks — part of a broader wave of Chinese model releases, including updates from ByteDance and DeepSeek, that's rapidly narrowing the gap with U.S. labs on both capability and price.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: More competition usually means lower prices trickle down to you — keep an eye on whether your AI vendor's pricing moves in response.
Source: Bloomberg

PRACTICAL TAKEAWAY

This week proved EU AI Act enforcement isn't hypothetical anymore — if your business uses any AI-driven scoring, screening, or decision tool, even a vendor's, start documenting how it works before a regulator asks. Separately, take ten minutes to check whether Gemini's new low-cost Flash tiers or Grok's new voice pricing could replace a task you're currently paying more for elsewhere — cheap, capable AI is arriving faster than most small businesses are taking advantage of it.
