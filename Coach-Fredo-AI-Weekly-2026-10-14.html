This week the big AI companies stopped selling chatbots and started selling coworkers: Google unveiled an agent with its own email address, OpenAI put buttons and calculators inside ChatGPT, and Anthropic slashed small-model prices. Microsoft's CEO then added the other half of the story, saying we should assume advanced models can be compromised. For a small business, that means more doing, so more guardrails.

— Coach Fredo

THE BIG SIX

ChatGPT / OpenAI

OpenAI began rolling out GPT-6 on October 7 with an "Intelligent UI" that puts tappable buttons, calculators, charts and editable graphs inside replies. GPT-6 Sol went to paid plans first, and GPT-6 Luna reached Free and Go users on October 8. Users can switch the visuals off, and turning on extended thinking disables them.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: Calculators and editable graphs inside the chat could save a quote or budget check a trip to a spreadsheet, but verify the numbers it shows.
Source: AI Weekly (citing TechCrunch)

Gemini / Google

At a Google Cloud event on October 8, Google unveiled a unified Gemini agent that takes objectives rather than instructions, plans the work, uses skills and tools, and can delegate to subagents. It gets its own Workspace account and email address, writes audit trails under its own name, and can run on models including Anthropic's Claude. Businesses get it first, consumers later, and no price was given.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: An agent with its own account is easy to audit; if you try one, give it limited access and review its work log weekly.
Source: TechCrunch

Claude / Anthropic

Anthropic launched Claude Haiku 5.5 on October 7-8, priced at $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output tokens for requests up to 100k tokens, and halved Sonnet 5.5 cache-read prices. Independent coverage notes the tokenizer produces about 30% more tokens than Haiku 4.5, so real savings will be smaller than the headline.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: If you pay per use through an app or API, cheaper small models can lower your monthly bill, so ask your tool vendor whether they will pass it on.
Source: AI Weekly

Manus

Manus reportedly raised more than $500 million in a round led by Boyu Capital and IDG Capital, with Tencent, HSG and ZhenFund also participating, per an October 11 report. The same report says Meta's proposed $2 billion acquisition was blocked by Chinese regulators, and Manus is back to operating independently. Valuation was not disclosed.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: If you rely on Manus agents, the funding is a good sign for staying power, but keep your own copies of important work and outputs.
Source: AF.NET

Microsoft

In an X post covered by The Verge on October 11, CEO Satya Nadella argued that advanced AI models should be assumed compromised. He called for an emergency brake, tamper-proof audit logs, and the ability to pause any model mid-task.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: You do not need a security team to copy this: keep logs of what your AI tools do, and make sure you can switch one off quickly.
Source: The Verge

Grok / xAI

Quiet week. I found no confirmed xAI announcement dated October 7-14. The latest dated roundup I could verify covers early September, including Grok Bot for Enterprise on September 3, and it leaves Grok 4.7's launch unconfirmed.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: Nothing new to act on; check xAI's own site before choosing Grok for client work.
Source: Big Hat Group
INTERNATIONAL

🇫🇷 France (Mistral)

Quiet week, with follow-up on last week's story: Mistral's Large 4, nicknamed "Le Chonk," was announced at an event in Abu Dhabi. CEO Artur Mensch said it is "above the Chinese models" in some areas but named no models or benchmarks. Public availability is set for October 27, and cybersecurity experts and government authorities get early access to a version with fewer safety restrictions.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: A strong European open-weight model is a useful option if you serve EU clients, but wait for independent tests after October 27.
Source: Reuters via DD India

🇪🇺 European Union

Quiet week on new rules. Context: a May 7 provisional deal on the Digital Omnibus moves the high-risk AI deadlines to December 2, 2027 for stand-alone systems and August 2, 2028 for AI in regulated products. Article 50 transparency duties were not delayed.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: If you use AI on customer-facing content in the EU, label it clearly and keep a simple list of the AI tools you use.
Source: White & Case

🇨🇳 China

Chinese mathematicians led by Shing-Tung Yau launched an alliance in Beijing on October 11, uniting eight institutions with a goal of 1,000-plus scientists building specialized AI models for math and formalized proofs. Yau said China lags the US in AI-driven frontier science. Separately, SemiAnalysis found only 3.6% of 857 Chinese AI releases included safety evaluations.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: Cheap Chinese models are tempting, but check safety documentation and data-handling terms before sending client data to them.
Source: SCMP

PRACTICAL TAKEAWAY

Pick one repetitive job this week, such as invoice reminders or quote follow-ups, and test it with an AI tool you already pay for. Give it narrow access, read everything it produces before a client sees it, and keep a log of what it did. If the vendor adds an agent, treat it like a new hire: limited access first, more trust once it earns it.
