This week's throughline is control — who gets to decide what AI you can access, and who's racing to make sure that decision isn't made for them. OpenAI, Google, and Microsoft are still playing pure scale-and-distribution, but Manus's split from Meta, Europe's new AI Act enforcement, and Mistral's sovereignty pitch all point the same direction: AI is becoming a policy question as much as a product one.

— Coach Fredo

THIS WEEK AT A GLANCE

  • OpenAI removed the text-chat cap for free ChatGPT users and made GPT-5.6 "Luna" the new default model. (TechCrunch)
  • Manus is splitting from Meta and resuming independent operations after Beijing blocked the $2B+ acquisition. (CNBC)
  • Microsoft 365 Copilot passed 30 million paid seats, with net new seats doubling quarter over quarter. (UC Today)
  • The EU began actively enforcing AI Act transparency rules, with fines up to €35 million for violations. (European Commission)
  • DeepSeek resumed its paused $8 billion funding round at a reported $74 billion valuation. (Bloomberg)
  • xAI opened a public beta of "Grok Bot," its internal automation tool, ahead of Grok 4.6. (x.AI)
THE BIG SIX

ChatGPT (OpenAI)

OpenAI lifted the cap on text-based conversations for free ChatGPT users and made GPT-5.6 "Luna" the new default model for Free and Go tiers, replacing GPT-5.5. The update also adds a "Think" button for tougher questions and folds in restaurant-booking help through OpenTable, Resy, and Yelp.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: Free-tier users now get essentially unlimited daily use, so if you've been testing ChatGPT on a no-cost account, you can lean on it harder before deciding whether a paid plan is worth it.
Source: TechCrunch

Gemini (Google)

Google moved Gemini 3.6 Flash and Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite out of preview into general availability this week, with 3.6 Flash offering better token efficiency and stronger coding and agentic planning at a lower price than its predecessor.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: Cheaper, faster "workhorse" models mean routine tasks like drafting, summarizing, and light coding get less expensive to automate without much of a quality trade-off.
Source: Google AI for Developers

Claude (Anthropic)

Anthropic is building an internal team dedicated to designing its own AI chips, aiming to co-design hardware and models together so Claude runs faster and more efficiently over time.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: This is a multi-year infrastructure bet, not something that changes your bill next month — but it's a signal Anthropic plans to be a long-term platform, which matters if you're building workflows on top of Claude.
Source: TechCrunch

Manus

Manus confirmed it will resume operating as an independent company after Meta's $2 billion-plus acquisition was unwound when Beijing blocked the deal on national security grounds. Data generated by certain users on or after December 29, 2025 will be deleted, and affected users have until August 23 to back it up.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: If your team uses Manus for agentic tasks, back up anything you need now — ownership uncertainty is exactly when tools change terms, pricing, or features with little warning.
Source: CNBC

Microsoft

Microsoft confirmed Copilot has passed 30 million paid seats, with net new seats more than doubling quarter over quarter and customers buying 50,000+ seats up more than sevenfold year over year. Copilot also picked up new features this week, including one-click SharePoint dashboards generated straight from a list.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: Enterprise AI adoption is compounding fast — if competitors your size are weaving Copilot into daily work, that's a good prompt to reassess your own Microsoft 365 setup before the gap widens.
Source: UC Today

Grok (xAI)

xAI opened a public beta of "Grok Bot," an internal automation tool it originally built for its own team, with Elon Musk teasing a wider rollout tied to this week's Grok 4.6 release.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: Watch this one rather than adopt it yet — it's an internal tool going public, not a mature product, so expect rough edges before it's ready for daily business use.
Source: x.AI
INTERNATIONAL

🇫🇷 France

Mistral is getting a fresh wave of attention as "Europe's answer" to US AI dependence, months after a brief US Commerce Department order temporarily cut off non-US access to a rival's most powerful model over security concerns. That episode reignited political support in France and the EU for building AI that doesn't depend on decisions made in Washington.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: If your business relies entirely on US-based AI tools, this is a reminder that access can be a policy decision, not just a product decision — worth knowing exactly which providers you depend on and why.
Source: Fortune

🇪🇺 European Union

The European Commission and national regulators began actively enforcing the EU AI Act's transparency rules on August 2 — chatbots must disclose they're AI, deepfakes and AI-generated content need machine-readable labels, and violations can bring fines up to €35 million or 7% of global revenue.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: If you sell to EU customers or use AI-generated content — chatbots, marketing images, voiceovers — in EU-facing channels, you need clear AI disclosure now; this isn't optional guidance anymore, it's active enforcement.
Source: European Commission

🇨🇳 China

DeepSeek restarted its paused $8 billion funding round this week, reportedly at a $74 billion valuation, with investors including China's national AI fund, NetEase, and JD.com in talks to join.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: DeepSeek's cheap, capable models have been a price-pressure force on the whole industry; a war chest this size likely keeps that pressure on AI pricing going, which is good news if you're shopping for budget-friendly AI tools.
Source: Bloomberg

PRACTICAL TAKEAWAY

This week's takeaway: know your dependency chain. List which AI tools your business actually relies on day to day, then check who owns them, where they're based, and what happens if access changes overnight (see Manus). You don't need a backup for every tool, but the one you use daily deserves a Plan B.
