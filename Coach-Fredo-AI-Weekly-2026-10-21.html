This week the AI headlines split in two. Prices keep dropping and the tools keep getting more capable, but regulators, state attorneys general and privacy reporters are asking harder questions about the biggest vendors. For a small business, that is a reminder to enjoy the savings and still know exactly what customer data you feed these tools.

— Coach Fredo

THIS WEEK AT A GLANCE

  • Google launches Gemini 4 Argon at $2 / $10 per million tokens (AI Weekly (Google blog item))
  • Eight state attorneys general sue OpenAI over ChatGPT child-safety claims (Bloomberg Law)
  • EU AI Office reportedly opens first AI Act probes into OpenAI and Anthropic (AI Weekly)
  • Anthropic IPO valuation talk cools to $1.5T as Q3 growth slows (Reuters)
  • xAI ships Grok Voice 3.0 at $0.08 per minute (AI Weekly)
  • DeepSeek open-sources DeepInfer, its production inference stack (GitHub via AI Weekly)
THE BIG SIX

ChatGPT (OpenAI)

Attorneys general in eight states (Arkansas, Arizona, Idaho, Montana, Iowa, Kansas, West Virginia and Nebraska) filed consumer-protection suits on October 14, alleging ChatGPT was marketed as safe for children under 13. Separately, 404 Media reported that ChatGPT's new ad platform can target people by the prompts they type and by uploaded phone numbers or emails.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: If your team uses ChatGPT, treat it as a public-facing tool: keep customer names, phone numbers and emails out of prompts and check your privacy and ad settings.
Source: Bloomberg Law via AI Weekly

Gemini (Google)

Google launched Gemini 4 Argon on October 13 at $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, with cached inputs 95% cheaper. Google says it ranks first on the Vals Index for coding, finance and legal work.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: Lower per-token prices make it cheaper to automate routine drafting and summarizing, so it is worth re-testing one task you shelved as too expensive. (Benchmark claims are Google's own.)
Source: AI Weekly

Claude (Anthropic)

Reuters reports that investors at Anthropic's October 14 investor day discussed a $1.5 trillion valuation for a planned November share sale, down from roughly $2 trillion floated earlier. The report puts Q3 revenue at $16.8 billion, up 45% from the prior quarter but slower than Q2.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: This is investor news, not a product change, but it is a good prompt to avoid depending on any single AI vendor for a core process.
Source: Reuters via AI Weekly

Manus

Quiet week: we found no new Manus announcement in the past seven days. Manus now describes itself as part of Meta, and Meta's updated commercial terms take effect October 30; Nieman Lab reports legal experts say they let Meta crawl sites tied to business accounts for AI training with no opt-out.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: If your business has Facebook, Instagram or WhatsApp business accounts, read the October 30 terms before they apply to your website content.
Source: Nieman Lab via AI Weekly

Microsoft

Microsoft's September 25 move to brand Copilot the "OS for work" pairs per-user subscriptions with usage-based credits, according to an October 5 report. Microsoft also held its Copilot + OneDrive digital event on October 20; we found no post-event coverage yet.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: Usage-based credits mean your Microsoft 365 AI bill can vary month to month, so ask your reseller how credits are metered before rolling Copilot out to staff.
Source: AF.net (republishing Cryptobriefing)

Grok (xAI)

xAI shipped Grok Voice 3.0, priced at $0.08 per minute, which AI Weekly's headline says tops a voice-quality index at 86.9. It is aimed at high-volume conversational calls.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: At eight cents a minute, AI phone answering for appointments or after-hours calls is now a line item a solo business can price out. Test quality before trusting it with customers.
Source: AI Weekly
INTERNATIONAL

🇫🇷 France

Mistral announced Mistral Large 4 on October 7 (just before this week's window) and says it will be publicly available October 27. Reuters reports the company has raised €3 billion and claims the model beats some rival open-weight models.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: A European model option matters if your customers care where their data is processed. Watch for the October 27 release and test it on one task.
Source: Reuters via DD India

🇪🇺 European Union

An AI Weekly headline reports the EU AI Office has opened its first AI Act probes, into OpenAI and Anthropic. We only saw the headline, so details are not confirmed here.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: Even if you are not in Europe, AI Act enforcement tends to shape vendor policies worldwide. Keep a simple record of which AI tools you use and what data goes in.
Source: AI Weekly

🇨🇳 China

DeepSeek open-sourced DeepInfer under the Apache 2.0 license on GitHub. DeepSeek describes it as the inference framework behind its large-scale production services, targeting its V4.1-Flash model on Nvidia and Huawei Ascend chips.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: Open-source tooling like this tends to push the cost of running AI down for everyone. If you use AI through a vendor, expect more price competition.
Source: GitHub via AI Weekly

PRACTICAL TAKEAWAY

Spend 30 minutes this week on an AI tool audit: list every AI tool your team uses, what customer data goes into it, and whether you have opted out of ad targeting and training where you can. Then pick one routine task, such as email drafts or call summaries, and re-test it on a cheaper model, since prices fell again this week.
