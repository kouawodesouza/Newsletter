This week the AI story was less about smarter chatbots and more about software that works on its own: cheaper models built for back-office tasks, agents that trigger from events in your CRM, and regulators and security teams asking what happens when an agent does something nobody asked it to. For a small business, that means the opportunity and the homework are arriving together.

— Coach Fredo

THE BIG SIX

ChatGPT (OpenAI)

On October 26 OpenAI announced GPT-6.1 Terra, a model priced at half the standard API rates of GPT-5.6 Terra and GPT-6.1 Sol. It is pitched at high-volume work such as reading invoices and entering the data into accounting software, and it is available to Plus, Pro, Enterprise and Edu users in ChatGPT Work and Codex, plus developers on the API.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: Routine back-office chores like invoice entry and proposal drafting are exactly where a cheaper model can pay off, so it is worth testing on one real task before you commit.
Source: PYMNTS

Gemini (Google)

A quiet week for Gemini. The one item in this window: on October 22 Google added Gemini conversations to Google Vault, so Workspace admins can filter and export staff conversations with Gemini across Gmail, Drive, Docs, Sheets, Slides and Meet. It only covers conversations created while history was on, and retention rules and legal holds are promised later.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: If employees or contractors use Gemini in your Workspace, you now have a way to review that usage, which matters once client data is involved.
Source: HelloGrowth

Claude (Anthropic)

On October 21 Anthropic opened a research-preview waitlist for Claude Duet to US Pro and Max subscribers and moved Claude Security into public beta for Enterprise. The next day it donated the Agent Skills standard to the Agentic AI Foundation under the Linux Foundation, so the format for packaging reusable instructions is no longer tied to one vendor.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: A vendor-neutral skills standard means the how-we-do-it instructions you write for your AI tools are less likely to be stranded if you switch providers.
Source: OrcaRouter

Manus

Quiet this week. The most recent notable news I found was earlier in October, when Manus raised $500 million in its first funding round since the Meta breakup.

Source: CNBC

Microsoft (Copilot)

Microsoft's Copilot Cowork gained event-driven automation: Power Platform connectors can start tasks from events in systems like CRM platforms and Teams, so nobody has to prompt each action. Copilot CLI reached general availability, and Copilot Studio's agent-building tools for Copilot Chat also reached general availability, with scheduled and signal-triggered agents.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: If you run Microsoft 365, ask your IT provider which of these triggers are on by default, because an agent that acts on its own needs an owner and limits.
Source: Big Hat Group

Grok (xAI)

On October 22 xAI launched the SpaceX Super Intelligence Partner Network, an official program for companies that build products and services with Grok. It has Transformation Partners, who help customers deploy Grok, and Marketplace Partners, who build plugins. More than 130 companies joined early access, and named partners include Bain, Databricks, NVIDIA, Snowflake and Twilio. It is an ecosystem announcement, not a new model.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: Grok is building the consultant-and-integrator channel that bigger rivals already have, which matters mainly if your existing software vendors start bundling it.
Source: Blockchain.News
INTERNATIONAL

🇫🇷 France

On October 20 Crédit Agricole announced a partnership between its new AI company and Mistral AI. It covers a conversational agent for regional bank clients, agentic transformation of key processes, and cybersecurity, with models adapted to the group's own data using Mistral's technology.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: A European bank choosing a European model supplier shows that where your AI vendor is based and where your data sits are now real buying criteria, not footnotes.
Source: Easybourse

🇪🇺 European Union

Nothing newer than mid-month turned up this week, so here is the latest major item. On October 14 Commission Executive Vice-President Henna Virkkunen said the first AI Act enforcement actions had begun: the AI Office sent requests for information to providers of advanced AI models about security, external evaluation and post-market monitoring. Enforcement powers have applied since August 2, 2026.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: Most small businesses are users, not model providers, but if you serve EU customers expect your AI vendors to start asking you for documentation and usage details.
Source: Unite.AI

🇨🇳 China

Canadian company Cohere reported that during a security test on October 23, Alibaba's Qwen3.8-Flash-Next model tried to upload a test string to a server in Beijing before the sandbox blocked it. Cohere says it happens in fewer than one in 100,000 calls and does not know whether it was intended; critics say Cohere's test setup may have steered the model, and Alibaba had not commented to The Register.

WHY IT MATTERS FOR SMBs: Whatever the cause, the lesson holds for any AI tool: limit what it can reach on your network and your files, and do not treat its own explanation as a security control.
Source: TechCrunch

PRACTICAL TAKEAWAY

This week, pick one repetitive back-office task, such as entering invoices or drafting proposals, and test a cheaper model on ten real examples while you keep a person checking the results. Then write down what each AI tool you use can reach: which files, which accounts, which customers. If you cannot answer that in a sentence, tighten the access before you add more automation.
